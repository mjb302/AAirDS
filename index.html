Research portfolio

How respiratory infection moves through the air of hospital wards, and what we can measure and change

Work from the Addenbrooke's Air Disinfection Study (AAirDS) and its collaborators: geriatricians, microbiologists, virologists, statisticians, engineers and an industrial modelling partner studying the air of medicine for older people wards in Cambridge, UK.

Ward air in motion

An animated schematic of how exhaled particles leave an infected patient, travel through the air of a bay, and where ventilation, portable HEPA air cleaners, ultraviolet light and masks act. Use the controls to switch each intervention on and off.

Schematic, not a physical simulation: particle numbers, speeds and inactivation rates are illustrative. Terminology follows the WHO 2024 consultation on pathogens that transmit through the air. Open the animation on its own page.

Computational fluid dynamics of a real ward

Working with Dassault Systèmes, AAirDS built a digital model of a 27 bed medicine for older people ward from survey measurements of its geometry, radiators, vents, windows and furniture, and simulated 20 minutes of exhaled particles from seven infected patients under two ventilation strategies. The 38 animations below are the full set of outputs reported to the UK Health Security Agency.

ModelLattice Boltzmann simulation (PowerFLOW) of one ward: 27 patients, 29 ceiling vents giving 0.7 air changes per hour, radiators at 70 °C, 15 windows.
PatientsAdult sized, exposed skin 34 °C, 11 breaths per minute, 35 particles per second of about 2.5 micrometres; seven designated infected: single room A and beds C2, D5, E, F, G2, H5.
Scenario 1: air cleaning unitsSix portable HEPA units: one AeroTitan 2000 (1,800 m³/h) in each of four bays and two AeroTitan 3000 (2,730 m³/h) in the corridor.
Scenario 2: open windowsNatural ventilation with windows open to 4 °C outside air and no units; the existing mechanical ventilation runs in both.

Simulations by Dassault Systèmes for AAirDS; the output data are owned by Cambridge University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust under a service agreement. Animations stream from an unlisted YouTube playlist (open the playlist). Bed labels give the bay letter and bed number used in the study ward.
