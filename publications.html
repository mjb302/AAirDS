What the programme has published

Peer reviewed papers from AAirDS and related work by members of the group, with a sentence on what each showed. Citation numbers follow the New Horizons review.

AAirDS outputs

2025

Efficacy of air cleaning units for preventing SARS-CoV-2 and other hospital-acquired infections on medicine for older people wards: a quasi-experimental controlled before-and-after study

Brock RC, Goudie RJB, Peters C, et al. J Hosp Infect 2025;155:1-8. doi:10.1016/j.jhin.2024.09.017

The main trial result. Among 6,112 patients (median age 85) on four wards over a year, portable HEPA/UV-C units were associated with a non-significant trend towards lower ward-acquired SARS-CoV-2 (hazard ratio 0.90, 95% CI 0.53 to 1.52). The study was underpowered, ran during the pandemic, and unit use fell over time.

2025

The role of spatial distance in SARS-CoV-2 nosocomial transmission

Illingworth CJR, Yahiaoui G, Butler M, et al. J Hosp Infect 2025;166:71-81. doi:10.1016/j.jhin.2025.09.011

Combined the CFD model with genomic and epidemiological reconstruction of transmission on the same wards. Exposure fell by roughly 40% to 50% for each metre of separation and four-fold in a single room, yet about 28% of transmission events could not be explained by distance, implying shared spaces and staff or visitor mediated routes.

2025

Acceptability of air cleaning units on inpatient wards: help for infection control or hindrance for ward occupants?

Love C, Street A, Riddell E, et al. J Hosp Infect 2025;155:254-256. doi:10.1016/j.jhin.2024.09.010

Patients, visitors and staff rated the units' noise as a moderate disturbance and most did not clearly link them to infection control. Staff engagement, more than patient tolerance, may determine whether units stay switched on.

2024

Addenbrooke's Air Disinfection Study: final report to the UK Health Security Agency

AAirDS study group. Cambridge University Hospitals NHS Foundation Trust; 2024.

The full report, including the CFD methods and all 38 animations in this gallery, the particulate and carbon dioxide sensor network, and the molecular surveillance of air and surfaces. Available from the study team on request.

2023

Impact of supplementary air filtration on aerosols and particulate matter in a UK hospital ward: a case study

Butler MJ, Sloof D, Peters C, et al. J Hosp Infect 2023;135:81-89. doi:10.1016/j.jhin.2023.02.006

The commissioning study. When a single unit was accidentally switched off for about seven hours, particle counts rose across the ward and fell when it was restored; signals rose and fell together across bays, single rooms and corridor, showing rapid movement of aerosol between ward compartments.

Related work by members of the group

2022

The removal of airborne SARS-CoV-2 and other microbial bioaerosols by air filtration on COVID-19 surge units

Conway Morris A, Sharrocks K, Bousfield R, et al. Clin Infect Dis 2022;75(1):e97-e101. doi:10.1093/cid/ciab933

On a Cambridge surge ward a portable HEPA filter with ultraviolet sterilisation removed detectable airborne SARS-CoV-2, which returned when it was switched off; filtration also reduced other microbial bioaerosols on that ward and a surge intensive care unit.

2022

Airborne protection for staff is associated with reduced hospital-acquired COVID-19 in English NHS trusts

Lawton T, Butler M, Peters C. J Hosp Infect 2022;120:81-84. doi:10.1016/j.jhin.2021.11.018

Across English trusts, providing respiratory protective equipment to all staff caring for COVID-19 patients was associated with 21% lower odds of hospital-acquired infection in the Alpha wave and 33% lower in the Delta wave.

2021

Efficacy of FFP3 respirators for prevention of SARS-CoV-2 infection in healthcare workers

Ferris M, Ferris R, Workman C, et al. eLife 2021;10:e71131. doi:10.7554/eLife.71131

Replacing fluid resistant surgical masks with FFP3 respirators for staff on Cambridge COVID-19 wards was associated with a 52% to 100% reduction in the modelled risk of ward-acquired infection.

2021

Superspreaders drive the largest outbreaks of hospital onset COVID-19 infections

Illingworth CJR, Hamilton WL, Warne B, et al. eLife 2021;10:e67308. doi:10.7554/eLife.67308

Genomic reconstruction of hospital outbreaks in Cambridge: patients were more likely to be infected by other patients than by staff, and about a fifth of infected individuals accounted for four fifths of onward transmission.

2020

Rapid implementation of SARS-CoV-2 sequencing to investigate cases of health-care associated COVID-19: a prospective genomic surveillance study

Meredith LW, Hamilton WL, Warne B, et al. Lancet Infect Dis 2020;20(11):1263-1272. doi:10.1016/S1473-3099(20)30562-4

On-site sequencing let infection control teams compare viral genomes from patients and staff on the same wards within days.

2015

Environmental contamination and hospital-acquired infection: factors that are easily overlooked

Beggs C, Knibbs LD, Johnson GR, Morawska L. Indoor Air 2015;25(5):462-474. doi:10.1111/ina.12170

Why airborne and contact routes are linked: bed making releases bacteria-laden particles, settled organisms contaminate surfaces that are then touched, and sinks release organisms in splash and drain aerosol.

2008

The ventilation of multiple-bed hospital wards: review and analysis

Beggs CB, Kerr KG, Noakes CJ, Hathway EA, Sleigh PA. Am J Infect Control 2008;36(4):250-259. doi:10.1016/j.ajic.2007.07.012

How the ventilation and air distribution of multi-bed wards shape the concentration of airborne pathogens, and how little was known about the ventilation needed to prevent infection.
